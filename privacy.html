rSshTerm privacy policy

Last updated: 26 November 2026.

The short version

rSshTerm collects no data. The developer runs no server for the app, has no account system, and receives nothing from it: no analytics, no crash reports, no advertising identifiers, no tracking.

Your servers, passwords and keys

Everything you enter (servers, user names, passwords, private keys, the hosts you have trusted) is kept in one vault. The vault is encrypted on your iPhone with AES-256-GCM before it is stored anywhere. The sealed vault is kept on the iPhone and, if you leave iCloud sync on, in the private iCloud database of your own Apple ID. The developer has no access to either.

The key that seals the vault is random. It is wrapped by a key that never leaves this iPhone and by a key in your iCloud Keychain (end-to-end encrypted by Apple), so your other Apple devices can open the vault; optionally also by a master password only you know. After the first unlock it is kept in the iPhone's Keychain, behind Face ID if you turn that on.

SSH connections

SSH sessions go directly from your iPhone to the servers you connect to. Nothing passes through a server of the developer.

Camera, microphone and dictation

The camera is used only when you take a photo to upload to a server or scan text into the terminal; the photo goes only to the server you chose. The microphone and speech recognition are used only while you dictate; recognition runs on the device where the iPhone supports it, otherwise Apple's speech service processes the audio. You review the text before anything is sent.

Local network and storage you add

A network scan looks for SSH servers on your local network only when you start it. S3 buckets and other file locations you add are contacted directly with the credentials you entered, which stay in the vault.

Desktop apps

The Linux and macOS apps follow the same rules. They also check for updates by downloading a public, signed version file from the rSshTerm release site (no identifier is sent), and, only if you turn it on, keep the sealed vault in your own Google Drive app folder.

Third parties

Only Apple's iCloud (your own private database and iCloud Keychain), your own SSH servers and the storage you add are contacted (plus, on the desktop, the release site and Google Drive if you turn it on). Apple's privacy policy applies to what iCloud stores for you.

This website

The rSshTerm website counts page views with Umami: no cookies, no personal data, no cross-site tracking; visits are counted anonymously. The apps themselves send nothing to it.

Children

The app is a tool for administering servers and is not directed at children.
